What we look at

What goes into a tax plan?

Every plan starts from your real numbers. These are the areas we look at most.

Business structure

Sole proprietor, LLC, S corporation or C corporation. An S corporation is a company that elects to pass its income through to the owners’ personal returns.

How you pay yourself

S corporation owners who work in the business must pay themselves a reasonable salary. The split between salary and distributions affects your tax.

Retirement plans

A retirement plan can lower taxable income now and build savings for later. We compare the options for you and your team.

Timing

When you buy equipment, collect income or pay expenses can change which year they count in.

Estimated taxes

We project what you are likely to owe and set quarterly estimated payments, so April holds no surprise.

Deductions and credits

We look at the deductions and credits that fit your situation, so you claim what you qualify for.

Timing matters

When should you start tax planning?

Start before December 31. Most planning moves have to happen within the tax year. Once the year ends, your return can only report what already happened.

The best time is mid year, when there are still months to act. The second best time is now.

Florida and federal rules

How do Florida and federal tax rules affect your plan?

Florida has no state personal income tax, so most planning for Florida residents is about federal income tax and self-employment tax. Self-employment tax is the Social Security and Medicare tax that self-employed people pay on business profit.

No state income taxFlorida does not tax the personal income of its residents, so a Florida resident files a federal income tax return only.

C corporationsA C corporation doing business in Florida files a Florida corporate income tax return, Form F-1120.

Estimated paymentsFederal estimated tax payments are generally due in April, June, September and January.

Pass-through incomeProfit from an S corporation or partnership passes through to the owners and is taxed on their personal returns.

New businesses

Can tax planning help a new business?

Yes, and the start is the best time for it. No business owner should have to work out these choices alone.

  • Entity type analysis. We help you choose the structure that fits your business, and we handle the state and federal forms.
  • Accounting system setup. We set up your accounting system so the books stay clean from the first month.
  • Retirement plan analysis. We help you find a plan that works for you and your employees and fits your budget.

What should you do first?

Bring last year’s tax return and your profit and loss report for this year to date. If you own a business, note any big purchases, hires or changes you are planning. Those plans shape the tax plan.

How is tax planning priced?

Questions

Questions about tax planning

Is tax planning legal?

Yes. Tax planning uses choices the tax law already gives you, such as retirement plans, business structure and the timing of income and expenses. We do not use schemes that hide income.

Can you promise I will pay less tax?

No one can promise that, because every situation is different. What we can do is show you your options and the likely tax effect of each one before you decide.

Should my LLC elect to be taxed as an S corporation?

It depends on your profit, your salary and your plans. An S corporation election can lower self-employment tax for some owners, but it adds payroll and a separate business return. We run the numbers for your situation before you file anything.

What retirement plans can a small business owner use?

Common options include a SEP IRA, a SIMPLE IRA and a 401(k), including a solo 401(k) for an owner with no employees. Each has its own rules and contribution limits, and we help you compare them.

When are federal estimated taxes due?

For most people, federal estimated tax payments are due April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15 of the next year. When a date falls on a weekend or holiday, it moves to the next business day.
